## Section 1 — Foundations of the British Parliamentary Framework [OPPONENT-INDEPENDENT]

### The BP Team Structure

| Team Position | Side | Speaker Roles |
|---|---|---|
| Opening Government (OG) | Affirmative | Prime Minister (PM), Deputy Prime Minister (DPM) |
| Opening Opposition (OO) | Negative | Leader of Opposition (LO), Deputy Leader of Opposition (DLO) |
| Closing Government (CG) | Affirmative | Member of Government, Government Whip |
| Closing Opposition (CO) | Negative | Member of Opposition, Opposition Whip |

### The Ethos of BP Debate

The foundational principle is the **"Ordinary Intelligent Person" standard**. Judges act as informed citizens — they may "gut-check" logic and dismiss premises that fail basic plausibility.

- **Higher evidentiary burden:** You cannot simply state a premise; you must fully mechanize its truth.
- **Isolation is the death of an argument.** An unconnected claim is a losing claim.

### Scoring and Ranking

- **Rankings:** 1st (3 points), 2nd (2 points), 3rd (1 point), 4th (0 points)
- **Speaker Points:** 0–100 scale
- **The Break:** Teams averaging ~2nd place across five rounds qualify for elimination bracket

## Section 2 — The Strategic Mindset: Tipping the Scale and Path to Victory [OPPONENT-DEPENDENT]

> ⚠️ **Do not use this section unless OG and OO content is known.** You cannot advise CG/CO on "tipping the scale" without knowing what weights OG and OO have placed.

### Tipping the Scale Metaphor

Every argument, characterization, and rebuttal is a weight on a physical scale. Your objective is to manipulate the scale until it decisively favors your side.

To command the scale, answer three questions:
1. **What is on the scale?** Define the content. Establish the two distinct worlds the judge is weighing.
2. **How much does it weigh?** This is characterization. Convince the judge your impacts are "heavier" than the opposition's.
3. **Why does it tip?** Identify the tipping point. Provide the specific reason why your logic moves the scale from equilibrium to victory.

### Path to Victory (PTV)

A PTV is a tactical blueprint employing "Three-Step Ahead" anticipatory logic:

> "We are going to win the debate by explaining why [Metric X] occurs and weighing the harms of that outcome over any competing arguments presented by the opposition in this round."

### Metrics

A metric is the judge's target — a quantifiable or tangible real-world outcome:
- **Economic Growth:** Internalizing externalities for measurable expansion
- **Reduced Trauma:** Demonstrating a shift in how stakeholders process experience
- **Empowerment:** Showing a specific stakeholder gains agency

Once established, your case must function as the **exclusive mechanism** to achieve it.

## Section 3 — Narrative Construction: The Four Pillars [PARTIALLY OPPONENT-DEPENDENT]

### The Four-Part Debate Story [OPPONENT-INDEPENDENT]

Human psychology resists half-baked data but is moved by structured stories. An argument must be a complete narrative.

1. **Establishing Stakes (The Problem):** Use framing to make stakes as high as possible. If the judge doesn't care about the causes, they won't value your solution.
2. **Mechanisms (The Solution):** Prove **Solvency** (policy works) and **Exclusivity** (benefit only exists on your side).
3. **Outcomes (The Impacts):** Impacts must be proportional to mechanisms. Claiming a minor policy solves global poverty is "undercooked."

### Weighing (The Resolution) [OPPONENT-DEPENDENT]

This is the first order of business — explain why your outcome is the most important factor in the round. **Requires knowing the opposition's impacts to compare against.**

### Resilience Frameworks [OPPONENT-INDEPENDENT]

| Structure Type | Metaphor | Resilience Level |
|---|---|---|
| Stepwise Story | The Domino Chain | Low. If opposition removes one link, the argument collapses. |
| Layered Story | The Hamburger | High. Even if opposition "takes out the cheese," the patty and bun remain. Multiple mechanisms ensure the argument stands. |

## Section 4 — Tactical Prep: The 15-Minute Blueprint [OPPONENT-INDEPENDENT]

### The Prep Blueprint

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| Minutes 0–3 | **Silent Brainstorming.** Prep the opposition first. You cannot win a fight if you don't know what you are fighting. |
| Minutes 3–6 | **Partner Synchronization.** Identify the Metric and select 1–2 "Round Winning" arguments. |
| Minutes 6–12 | **Case Completion.** Construct the four-part story with layered mechanisms. |
| Minutes 12–15 | **The "Bad Cop" Stress Test.** Switch mindsets. If you were the opponent, where would you "stab holes"? Patch logical gaps with preemptive logic. |

### The "Galactic Brain" Trap

Avoid the "Hail Mary" of complex, non-intuitive leaps. Elite teams win by executing **"Stock/Cookie Cutter" arguments** — social justice, finance, movement politics — with superior depth. These are not "boring"; they are stable foundations. A well-executed stock argument always beats an undercooked "Galactic Brain" failure.

## Section 5 — Rhetorical Architecture: PM and LO Introduction [OPPONENT-INDEPENDENT]

### The Three Rhetorical Buckets

| Bucket | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Ethos** (Credibility) | Establish authority | Mentioning relevant internship/experience before a topic |
| **Pathos** (Emotion) | Make the judge care | Using culturally resonant narratives to illustrate trauma |
| **Logos** (Logic) | Ground in reality | Citing statistics on poverty, policy outcomes, etc. |

### Linguistic Dominance

Language is socially constructed. Choose **prescriptive terms** that shift the judge's perception before technical arguing begins.

| Pejorative/Negative Term | Prescriptive/Positive Transformation |
|---|---|
| Sledgehammer | Pickaxe (Precision) |
| Cult of Productivity | Proactive Lifestyle |
| Military Intervention | Humanitarian Relief |
| Laziness | Anti-grind Culture |
| Leaky Bucket (Immigration) | Resilience of Human Movement |

Setting these terms early allows you to define the battlefield through strategic modeling.

## Section 6 — Defining the Battlefield: Strategic Modeling [OPPONENT-INDEPENDENT]

### Motion Types

| Type | Requirement | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Policy Motions (THW)** | Require a specific **Model**. OO may Counter-model if mutually exclusive. | "THW ban social media" |
| **Analysis Motions (THB)** | Require **Characterization**. Use Likelihood Analysis to argue actor incentives. | "THB that capitalism has failed" |

### The Strategic Modeling Checklist

- **Timeline:** Phase-in vs. Immediate (phasing avoids transitory shocks)
- **Punishment:** Harshness levels (clear bright lines prevent black market seepage)
- **Scope:** Global vs. Regional
- **Definition:** Specific inclusions (e.g., "cigarettes" includes vapes)

By characterizing actor incentives and political landscapes, you ensure the debate happens in a world favorable to your side.

## Section 7 — Logical Rigor: Burdens of Proof and Mechanization [OPPONENT-INDEPENDENT]

### Addressing Burdens of Proof

For any argument, you must answer the "Natural Questions" a judge will have:
- Why does the problem exist now?
- Why is the ban needed over the comparative of regulation?
- How do actors respond to this specific incentive?

### Mechanization and Impact Quantification

A mechanism is a **specific, unique reason** to believe a claim. For the classic "Bans Create Black Markets" argument, you must prove:
1. **Comparative:** Life is worse underground than in a regulated market
2. **Solvency:** The ban fails to stop the behavior
3. **Uniqueness:** The harm is exclusive to the ban

Use **Impact Quantification** ("Debate Math") to make these concrete. If an opponent cannot quantify their impact and you can, you win the scale by default.

### The Leader of Opposition (LO) Checklist

The LO must conduct **Substructure Sabotage** rather than just rebuttal:
- Integrate rebuttal into your own constructive burdens
- Show your mechanisms are more plausible than OG's
- Capture the framing battle early to make OG's points irrelevant

### Mastery Homework

"Judge the Judge." Watch world-class rounds, prep the motion, and compare your rankings to the panel's. When your logic aligns with the actual decision, you have mastered the Architect's approach.
