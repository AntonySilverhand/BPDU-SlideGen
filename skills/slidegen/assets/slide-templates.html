Motion Analysis

Defining the Motion

Break down the key terms of the motion. Each card unpacks one word or phrase that carries definitional weight.

"This House"

The collective actor in the debate. Represents a government, institution, or reasonable deliberative body — not a single person. Its decisions are binding in the world of the debate.

"Would"

A policy motion — the debate is about whether a proposed action should be taken. Government must defend a specific mechanism; opposition must offer a counter or defend the status quo.

"Key Phrase"

Define precisely. Ambiguity here gives the closing bench room to construct an extension. Opening Government should own this definition in the PM speech and defend it throughout.

Say "Yes" or "I'll take your point". The interjection must be brief — 15 seconds maximum.

Accept 1–2 per speech. Too few or too many both signal weakness. Quality counts over quantity.

The Extension Rule

What Is an Extension?

The single most misunderstood rule in BP debate. Closing teams must add, not repeat.

Must Be New

A closing bench extension must introduce a distinct argument not made by the opening bench on the same side. Repetition of opening arguments will not be rewarded.

Must Be Different

The extension can agree with opening's conclusion but must reach it via a different mechanism, impact, or framing — offering the bench a new reason to vote for the motion.

Must Coexist

An extension cannot directly contradict the opening bench on the same side. CG and OG must be able to stand simultaneously in the same world.

The test: Could a judge vote for the closing bench's argument even if they rejected the opening bench's case entirely? If yes — it's a genuine extension.

dark bg  |  .strategy-grid (grid 1fr 1fr) › .strat (.strat-tag.gov/.opp/.all + h3 + p)

Debate Strategy

Winning This Motion

Position-specific priorities. Know your role before you stand up.

Opening Government

Own the Definition

Set a tight, fair definition that benefits government but is defensible. A challenged definition costs the whole bench. Make your model concrete and simple.

Opening Opposition

Challenge the Framing

Don't just rebut — offer a counter-model. Judges want to vote for a vision, not just a refusal. Give them a positive reason to side with opposition.

Closing Government

Genuine Extension Only

Find a new argument, new stakeholder, or new mechanism. Repetition of OG will not be rewarded no matter how well-delivered. Ask: could this stand alone?

All Teams

Weigh Your Impacts

Tell judges which harm matters most and why. Scale, severity, reversibility, and distribution are the four axes of impact comparison. Make the comparison explicit.
